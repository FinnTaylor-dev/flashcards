---

Q: What is the focus of OCR Computer Science A Level topic 1.5.2?
A: Moral, social, ethical and cultural opportunities and risks of digital technology.

---

Q: What areas of digital technology are covered in topic 1.5.2?
A: Computers in the workforce; automated decision making; artificial intelligence; environmental effects; censorship and the Internet; monitoring behaviour; analysing personal information; piracy and offensive communications; and website layout, colour paradigms and character sets.

---

Q: What are ethics concerned with?
A: A community’s values and how they affect different groups of people in society.

---

Q: What are morals concerned with?
A: A person’s individual code of conduct and how they choose to behave, including decisions made at the expense of others.

---

Q: What considerations arise because computers are integral to everyday life?
A: The moral, ethical, environmental, social and cultural implications of technological change.

---

Q: What is the first step towards resolving issues caused by technological change?
A: Identifying the issues.

---

Q: Why has the impact of computers on jobs become a concern?
A: Almost all businesses rely on computers for some functions, while the effects and likelihood of a completely computerised society remain uncertain.

---

Q: How can computers improve efficiency in the workforce?
A: They can reduce delivery times, speed up manufacturing, improve customer satisfaction and reduce unit labour costs, which can lower consumer prices.

---

Q: How do Amazon and Effidence illustrate the use of computers in the workforce?
A: Amazon uses warehouse robots to package and move goods between stations. Effidence’s PostBot navigates neighbourhoods to deliver letters and parcels door-to-door.

---

Q: How can computerised roles benefit workers?
A: They can reduce physical strain and the need to work in repetitive, tedious environments.

---

Q: What employment risk can result from computerisation?
A: Workers can lose their jobs, causing structural unemployment.

---

Q: How might cheaper computerised services create jobs elsewhere?
A: Lower prices may increase consumer demand, creating employment in other parts of a business.

---

Q: How can governments reduce the severity of job losses caused by computerisation?
A: By investing in retraining programmes that help people change occupations.

---

Q: Which jobs have been particularly affected by computerisation?
A: Middle-income manufacturing jobs, where robots may replace entire factory production lines.

---

Q: What employment shift can result from automation of manufacturing?
A: A shift towards low-income service jobs.

---

Q: Which occupations outside manufacturing have also been replaced by computers?
A: Examples include library assistants and taxi drivers.

---

Q: What does the replacement of library assistants and taxi drivers suggest about automation?
A: More jobs may be at risk than only repetitive jobs traditionally associated with automation.

---

Q: What is a major risk of a highly computerised workplace?
A: High dependence on computers: if systems fail, output can be greatly reduced.

---

Q: How can computerisation increase demand for computing-related workers?
A: Software developers are needed to create programmes for embedded systems, and network engineers are needed to maintain networks.

---

Q: What computer skills are employers increasingly seeking?
A: The ability to use computers confidently and productively.

---

Q: How can offering services exclusively online reduce business costs?
A: It can reduce the cost of renting physical premises and lower labour costs by requiring fewer front-facing retail workers and bank clerks.

---

Q: What are examples of services commonly offered exclusively online?
A: Online shopping and online banking.

---

Q: Which jobs may see increased demand as online services grow?
A: Delivery drivers to ship parcels and web developers to keep websites functioning.

---

Q: What is automated decision making?
A: The use of algorithms to make decisions, which can significantly affect people’s lives.

---

Q: How may algorithms decide what appears on a user’s social media feed?
A: They may use the user’s interactions and infer interests from connections and other information.

---

Q: What is a potential social risk of tailoring social media feeds to users’ interests?
A: It may reinforce existing beliefs in a “dangerous bubble,” where beliefs are not challenged, contributing to a close-minded society.

---

Q: What debate arises over the content of social media feeds?
A: Whether users should be shown important news stories or have their feeds tailored to their interests.

---

Q: How have algorithms been used in criminal justice?
A: In the US, they have been used to assess a criminal’s likelihood of committing another crime based on various factors.

---

Q: In which application processes have algorithms been used to assess suitability?
A: Applications for mortgages, loans and jobs.

---

Q: Why might relying entirely on algorithms in application or criminal justice decisions be unfair?
A: Algorithms may not account for extenuating circumstances or contextual factors as well as humans can.

---

Q: What are some benefits of automated decision making in recruitment?
A: It can improve productivity, make application processes more convenient and help companies hire workers faster by screening candidates before interviews.

---

Q: How can algorithmic decisions in driverless cars potentially save lives?
A: Algorithms can make decisions faster than humans can react.

---

Q: How is automated decision making used in stock-market trading?
A: It analyses past trends to model future changes and make rapid trading decisions.

---

Q: How can automated decision making help plant automation and power distribution systems?
A: The systems collect and analyse inputs, then respond instantly to changes, such as increasing power supply when demand is forecast to rise.

---

Q: What is needed for algorithms to produce high-quality decisions?
A: Thorough testing and factually correct data.

---

Q: Why can’t an algorithm automatically be assumed to be free of bias?
A: It is designed by software developers, whose choices may introduce bias.

---

Q: What safeguards can help make algorithmic decisions fair?
A: Test algorithms thoroughly for bias, use accurate data, make decisions explainable and justified rather than a “black box,” and include some human input.

---

Q: What ethical dilemma can arise in a driverless-car emergency?
A: Deciding who should be harmed if either a pedestrian or the driver must be harmed.

---

Q: What responsibility question follows from a harmful decision made by a driverless car?
A: Who is responsible for the consequences of the decision.

---

Q: What is artificial intelligence?
A: The ability of a computer to replicate human intelligence and cognitive ability, and to grasp abstract concepts.

---

Q: How can AI support medical research?
A: Expert systems can identify connections between illnesses and use them to form diagnoses.

---

Q: What is another name for an expert system?
A: A knowledge-based system.

---

Q: What does an expert system aim to replicate?
A: The knowledge and experience an expert has in a particular subject.

---

Q: What is a knowledge base in an expert system?
A: A set of facts and rules.

---

Q: What does an inference engine do in an expert system?
A: It uses the knowledge base and is interrogated to find diagnoses.

---

Q: What is a neural network?
A: An AI system that replicates a biological neural network.

---

Q: How do neural networks learn?
A: They learn from a set of data and apply what they learn to new data sets.

---

Q: What are two uses of neural networks?
A: Pattern detection and identifying financial fraud.

---

Q: Where are voice-recognition AI systems commonly used?
A: In smart-home systems, such as Google Home and Amazon’s Alexa.

---

Q: What convenience can voice-recognition smart-home systems provide?
A: They allow people to interact with smart-home technology using voice commands.

---

Q: What privacy concern is raised by voice-recognition smart-home systems?
A: They need to remain switched on to function.

---

Q: What accountability question does AI raise?
A: Who is responsible when something goes wrong?

---

Q: What ethical question could arise if AI became sentient?
A: What rights, if any, it should have.

---

Q: Why has the affordability of technological devices increased their environmental impact?
A: Cheap, widely available devices encourage people to buy and discard more devices while keeping up with trends.

---

Q: What toxic materials may be found in some computer components?
A: Mercury and radioactive isotopes.

---

Q: What environmental harm can result from toxic computer components?
A: They can contaminate water supplies.

---

Q: What is one ethical concern about disposal of e-waste?
A: It is often shipped to countries with lower environmental standards for disposal, which is considered immoral and unacceptable.

---

Q: How can increased computerisation affect electricity consumption?
A: More devices and computerised processes require more electricity.

---

Q: How can electricity use for technology contribute to global warming?
A: If electricity comes from non-renewable fossil fuels, generating it emits greenhouse gases that contribute to global warming.

---

Q: How can technology consumption affect future generations and biodiversity?
A: Its environmental impacts can persist and affect both future generations and biodiversity.

---

Q: What changes in consumer behaviour can reduce the environmental impact of technology?
A: Being more careful about consumption and reducing the throw-away attitude towards technological devices.

---

Q: How can renewable energy reduce the environmental impact of increased electricity use?
A: It can counteract some effects of increased electricity consumption.

---

Q: How can smart-home systems help reduce energy use?
A: Temperature sensors can control when heating switches on, and motion sensors can switch off lights when a room is empty.

---

Q: How can computers and laptops reduce electricity use when not actively in use?
A: They can use “Sleep” and “Stand-by” features.

---

Q: How can some car engines reduce emissions?
A: They are designed to prevent the car from idling.

---

Q: What is the overall potential of technology in relation to environmental effects?
A: Technology can offset some environmental impacts through energy-saving and environmentally friendly technologies.

---

Q: What is censorship?
A: Suppressing content that people are able to view, publish or access.

---

Q: How may governments misuse censorship?
A: They may block alternative political opinions to push a particular ideology rather than protect the public.

---

Q: What websites do UK internet service providers block, according to the notes?
A: Websites containing material associated with terrorism and extremist political beliefs.

---

Q: What is the concern about censorship of alternative political beliefs?
A: It could be used to promote a particular ideology, which some people consider unethical and unacceptable.

---

Q: What is meant by a “Free Internet”?
A: An Internet where nothing is filtered, in line with freedom of speech.

---

Q: What was Tim Berners-Lee’s vision for the Internet, as described in the notes?
A: A space where people could share resources and mutually benefit from them.

---

Q: Why do many people consider some Internet censorship necessary?
A: For national security and to filter offensive comments and extremist propaganda.

---

Q: How may censorship be used in schools?
A: To prevent pupils from accessing material considered unsuitable.

---

Q: Why might censorship be used in workplaces?
A: To maintain productivity and prevent distractions.

---

Q: How may employers monitor workers’ productivity?
A: By tracking the websites and applications they access and the time spent using them.

---

Q: What are common purposes of surveillance systems such as CCTV?
A: Security and detecting crime, including offences such as speeding and violence.

---

Q: How can CCTV support law enforcement?
A: It can help trace and punish criminal activity.

---

Q: What is an ankle monitor used for?
A: Tracking the location of a person under house arrest.

---

Q: What ethical arguments are made about ankle monitors?
A: Critics argue they are unethical and violate basic human rights; supporters argue they are necessary to deter crime.

---

Q: What is big data?
A: Large amounts of data collected from multiple sources.

---

Q: What is data mining?
A: Analysing data to make inferences, such as conclusions about people’s behaviour, likes and dislikes.

---

Q: What can data mining reveal about people?
A: Insights about their behaviour, preferences and connections between variables.

---

Q: What are three uses of data mining?
A: Personalised political propaganda, targeted advertising, and discovering previously unknown connections between variables.

---

Q: How could data mining insights harm an individual?
A: It might reveal that they are at high risk of a disease, leading an insurance company to deny coverage or charge a higher premium.

---

Q: What ethical question arises when companies uncover personal information through data mining?
A: Whether they have a responsibility to share that information with the people concerned.

---

Q: What are companies legally required to tell users about data collection?
A: What kinds of data they are collecting.

---

Q: Why may users still be unclear about companies’ data practices?
A: Information about what data is collected may not be obvious, and users may not understand how it will be used.

---

Q: What transparency are large firms pressured to provide about personal data?
A: Clearer information about the data they collect and how they will use it.

---

Q: What did the notes say about the Data Protection Act developed in 1998?
A: They described it as no longer sufficient to protect public welfare.

---

Q: When did the notes say the GDPR was enforced?
A: May 2018.

---

Q: What is piracy?
A: The unauthorised copying of content, such as software, music or films.

---

Q: Why is piracy illegal?
A: It is a form of theft.

---

Q: What ethical decision does online piracy raise?
A: Whether an individual considers unauthorised copying ethically acceptable, despite the Internet making content easier to obtain.

---

Q: What is offensive communication?
A: Online harassment, including cyber-bullying and stalking.

---

Q: Why might people engage in offensive online communication?
A: The Internet can provide a seemingly anonymous front.

---

Q: What did the Malicious Communications Act introduced in 1998 make a criminal offence?
A: Sending indecent or offensive messages to anyone online.

---

Q: Can offensive online communications be traced?
A: Yes. Law enforcement organisations can trace them, and the behaviour can result in a criminal record.

---

Q: What should web developers consider when designing a website?
A: Who will use it, and how to accommodate all users.

---

Q: What does the Equality Act introduced in 2010 make illegal, according to the notes?
A: Discriminating against a certain group of people by refusing to provide them with a service.

---

Q: What is an important goal of website layout?
A: Making it easy for users to navigate between pages.

---

Q: How do menu placement conventions differ between English-speaking and Arabic-speaking contexts?
A: Menus are commonly placed on the left in English-speaking countries; in places such as Egypt or the UAE, they may be on the right because Arabic is read right to left.

---

Q: Why should website layouts account for language and cultural conventions?
A: To make websites accessible to people from different backgrounds.

---

Q: How can a well-laid-out website benefit an online store?
A: Easy navigation can attract more customers.

---

Q: How can websites accommodate people with visual impairments or other disabilities?
A: They can allow text enlargement and contrast adjustment, provide alt text for images, and offer a screen magnifier.

---

Q: What is alt text?
A: Alternative text provided for images.

---

Q: How can websites accommodate people with hearing impairments?
A: By providing transcripts of audio files.

---

Q: Why should web developers consider how colours are interpreted around the world?
A: Colours can have different cultural meanings, including unlucky or negative associations.

---

Q: How does the meaning of white differ in the example given?
A: White is associated with mourning in the Middle East and with purity in Western cultures.

---

Q: What kind of colour scheme is typically chosen for a website?
A: A neutral scheme with widely positive connotations.

---

Q: What meanings are associated with green in the notes?
A: Luck and nature.

---

Q: Why should website content be translated into multiple languages?
A: To make websites accessible to as wide an audience as possible.

---

Q: Why might a character set fail to represent every character in a language?
A: It may be too small to accommodate all the characters used by that language.

---

Q: Why can ASCII not represent all characters in Chinese?
A: ASCII uses only seven bits, so it cannot represent all Chinese characters.

---

Q: Which character set is preferred for representing a wide range of languages?
A: Unicode.

---

Q: How many characters can Unicode represent, according to the notes?
A: Over a million characters.
