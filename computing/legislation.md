Q: Which four pieces of computing-related legislation are covered in the OCR A Level Computer Science specification?
A: The Data Protection Act 1998, the Computer Misuse Act 1990, the Copyright, Design and Patents Act 1988, and the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000.

---

Q: What types of storage does the Data Protection Act 1998 apply to?
A: Information stored both on computers and in organised paper filing systems.

---

Q: What is personal data under the Data Protection Act 1998?
A: Any data which can be used to identify a living person, including data which can be used in combination with other data to identify a living person.

---

Q: What is an example of personal data?
A: A person's name and address.

---

Q: What is the term for an individual who can be identified by personal data?
A: Data subject.

---

Q: In how many specific ways does the Data Protection Act 1998 protect data subjects?
A: Eight, as laid out by the Government.

---

Q: List the eight principles of the Data Protection Act 1998.
A:

Personal data shall be processed fairly and lawfully
Personal data shall be obtained only for one or more specified and lawful purpose
Personal data shall be adequate, relevant and not excessive for its purpose(s)
Personal data shall be accurate and where necessary kept up to date
Personal data shall not be kept for longer than is necessary for its purpose(s)
Personal data shall be processed in accordance with the rights of data subjects under the Act
Appropriate technical and organisational measures shall be taken against unauthorised or unlawful processing of personal data and against accidental loss or destruction of, or damage to, personal data
Personal data shall not be transferred to a country or territory outside the European Economic Area, unless that country or territory ensures an adequate level of data protection

---

Q: What rights does the Data Protection Act 1998 give data subjects?
A:

The right to request a copy of the data held about them
The right to correct the data held about them
The right to prevent marketing using contact details provided by the data subject

---

Q: As of 2018, what has replaced the Data Protection Act 1998?
A: The General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and the Data Protection Act 2018.

---

Q: What does CMA stand for?
A: The Computer Misuse Act 1990.

---

Q: What does the Computer Misuse Act 1990 concern?
A: The malicious use of computers.

---

Q: Why was the Computer Misuse Act 1990 originally created?
A: To ensure that computer hacking was covered by law, and it has since been frequently updated to ensure it remains relevant.

---

Q: What are the three primary offences outlined in the Computer Misuse Act 1990?
A:

Unauthorised access to computer materials
Unauthorised access with intent to commit further offences
Unauthorised modification of computer material

---

Q: What is an example of unauthorised access to computer materials?
A: Finding a teacher's password and opening their files.

---

Q: What is an example of unauthorised access with intent to commit further offences?
A: Finding a teacher's password and opening their files with the intention of increasing your mark on their markbook.

---

Q: What is an example of unauthorised modification of computer material?
A: Finding a teacher's password, opening their files and increasing your mark on their markbook.

---

Q: How do the consequences of the second and third offences under the Computer Misuse Act compare to the first?
A: The consequences of the second and third offences are generally worse than the first, with each offence being punishable with imprisonment.

---

Q: What was the Copyright, Design and Patents Act 1988 brought in to do?
A: To protect people's property online, covering everything from logos to music recordings.

---

Q: When does copyright apply to a work under the Copyright, Design and Patents Act 1988, and when does it expire?
A: If a work is original, copyright automatically applies and doesn't expire until 25 to 70 years after the death of the creator, depending on the type of work.

---

Q: How was the Copyright, Design and Patents Act extended in 1992?
A: It was extended to include computer programs as a type of literary work, meaning copyright automatically applies to code.

---

C: In 1992, the Copyright, Design and Patents Act was extended to include [computer programs] as a type of [literary work].

---

Q: If an individual believes their work has been copied, whose responsibility is it to take action under the Copyright, Design and Patents Act?
A: It is their responsibility to take action under the Act.

---

Q: What does RIPA stand for?
A: The Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000.

---

Q: What does the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act 2000 cover?
A: Investigation, surveillance and interception of communication by public bodies.

---

Q: Under what condition can the Secret Service legally wire-trap online conversations?
A: In the interests of national security, provided that a warrant has been issued.

---

Q: What obligations does the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act place on internet service providers and mobile phone companies?
A: They must give up information upon request from an authorised authority and ensure their networks have sufficient hardware installed to facilitate surveillance.

---

Q: Why is the Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act particularly controversial?
A: Its powers extend to small agencies like local councils, and some people feel it is an invasion of privacy or that it is often improperly used.
