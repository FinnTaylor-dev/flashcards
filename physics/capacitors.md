Q: What is a capacitor?
A: An electrical component that stores charge on two separated metallic plates.

---

C: A [capacitor] is an electrical component that stores charge on two separated metallic plates, with a [dielectric] placed between them to prevent charge from travelling across the gap.

---

Q: What is the purpose of the dielectric in a capacitor?
A: To prevent charge from travelling across the gap and to increase the capacitance by polarizing in the electric field.

---

Q: How is capacitance defined?
A: The charge stored per unit potential difference across the two plates.

---

C: The capacitance, $C$, is defined as the [charge stored, $Q$] per unit [potential difference, $V$], given by the formula [$C = \frac{Q}{V}$].

---

Q: What is the formula for capacitance?
A: $C = \frac{Q}{V}$

---

Q: What is the unit of capacitance?
A: Farad (F), which is equivalent to C V$^{-1}$.

---

Q: What happens to the charges on the plates when a capacitor is connected to a DC power supply?
A: The power supply draws electrons from one plate, leaving it with a charge of $+Q$, and deposits them on the other plate, giving it a charge of $-Q$.

---

Q: When does current stop flowing in a circuit with a charging capacitor?
A: When the potential difference between the plates equals the electromotive force (e.m.f.) of the power supply.

---

Q: What does the electrical permittivity of a dielectric describe?
A: Its ability to polarize and strengthen the charge storage capability of the capacitor.

---

Q: Which of Kirchhoff's laws is used to derive the formula for capacitors in series?
A: Kirchhoff's voltage law.

---

Q: What is the formula for the total capacitance ($C_T$) of capacitors connected in series?
A: $\frac{1}{C_T} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \frac{1}{C_3} + \dots$

---

C: For capacitors in [series], the total capacitance is found using $\frac{1}{C_T} = \frac{1}{C_1} + \frac{1}{C_2} + \dots$, which is mathematically similar to the formula for resistors in [parallel].

---

Q: Which of Kirchhoff's laws is used to derive the formula for capacitors in parallel?
A: Kirchhoff's current law.

---

Q: What is the formula for the total capacitance ($C_T$) of capacitors connected in parallel?
A: $C_T = C_1 + C_2 + C_3 + \dots$

---

Q: What does the area under a charge-potential difference ($Q$-$V$) graph for a capacitor represent?
A: The work done in charging the capacitor, which equals the energy stored.

---

C: The energy stored in a capacitor can be calculated using the formulas [$W = \frac{1}{2}QV$], [$W = \frac{1}{2}V^2C$], or [$W = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$].

---

Q: What are the three formulas for the energy ($W$) stored in a capacitor?
A: $W = \frac{1}{2}QV$, $W = \frac{1}{2}V^2C$, and $W = \frac{Q^2}{2C}$.

---

Q: Give three practical applications of capacitors.
A: Camera flashes, touch screens, and uninterruptible power supplies (UPSs).

---

Q: How do capacitors function in AC to DC smoothing circuits?
A: They store energy as the potential difference rises and discharge as it falls, maintaining a more constant current.

---

Q: Why does a capacitor discharge when connected to a resistor?
A: The electrons on the negative plate repel each other and flow through the circuit, dissipating energy as heat in the resistor until the potential difference across the plates is zero.

---

Q: What is the time constant ($\tau$) of a capacitor-resistor circuit?
A: The product of the capacitance and the resistance, $\tau = CR$.

---

C: The time constant of a capacitor-resistor circuit is given by [$\tau = CR$], and at $t = \tau$, the charge on a discharging capacitor decreases to approximately [37%] of its original value.

---

Q: What happens to the charge on a discharging capacitor after one time constant ($t = \tau$)?
A: It decreases to approximately 37% of its original value.

---

Q: What is the equation for the charge ($Q$) on a discharging capacitor at time $t$?
A: $Q = Q_0 e^{-t/CR}$

---

Q: What are the equations for voltage ($V$) and current ($I$) during capacitor discharge?
A: $V = V_0 e^{-t/CR}$ and $I = I_0 e^{-t/CR}$.

---

Q: What is the equation for the potential difference across a charging capacitor ($V_C$) at time $t$?
A: $V_C = V_0 (1 - e^{-t/CR})$

---

Q: For an exponential decay, what is the relationship between successive measurements of voltage at equal time intervals?
A: The ratio of successive values is constant, meaning $\frac{V_{i+1}}{V_i} = \frac{V_{i+2}}{V_{i+1}}$.

---

Q: What components are needed to experimentally investigate the discharge of a capacitor?
A: A DC power supply, a capacitor, a resistor in series, an ammeter in series, and a voltmeter in parallel.

---

Q: Why are data loggers often used when investigating capacitor discharge?
A: Because capacitors often discharge very quickly, requiring rapid data collection at small time intervals compared to the time constant.

Q: How does the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor depend on the area of the plates ($A$)?
A: It is directly proportional to the area ($C \propto A$).

---

Q: How does the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor depend on the separation of the plates ($d$)?
A: It is inversely proportional to the separation ($C \propto \frac{1}{d}$).

---

C: The capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor is [directly] proportional to the area of the plates ($A$) and [inversely] proportional to the separation of the plates ($d$).

---

Q: What is the formula for the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor with a vacuum between the plates?
A: $C = \frac{\varepsilon_0 A}{d}$

---

Q: What is the general formula for the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor with a dielectric?
A: $C = \frac{\varepsilon A}{d}$

---

Q: How is the permittivity of a material ($\varepsilon$) related to the permittivity of free space ($\varepsilon_0$)?
A: $\varepsilon = \varepsilon_r \varepsilon_0$, where $\varepsilon_r$ is the relative permittivity of the dielectric medium.
