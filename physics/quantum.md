Q: What provides evidence that electromagnetic radiation travels through space as a continuous wave?
A: Diffraction and interference.

---

Q: How does electromagnetic radiation interact with matter?
A: As discrete energy quanta called photons.

---

Q: What is the equation for the energy of a photon in terms of frequency?
A: $E = hf$

---

Q: What is the equation for the energy of a photon in terms of wavelength?
A: $E = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$

---

Q: What is the numerical value of the Planck constant, $h$?
A: $6.63 \times 10^{-34}$

---

Q: What is the definition of an electronvolt (eV)?
A: The energy transferred when an electron travels through a potential difference of 1 volt.

---

Q: What is the value of 1 electronvolt in joules?
A: $1.60 \times 10^{-19} \text{ J}$

---

Q: What formula is used to derive the value of 1 electronvolt in joules?
A: $W = VQ$

---

Q: Why does an LED only emit light when the potential difference across it exceeds a certain threshold?
A: Because a minimum energy is required for an electron to lose energy and emit a photon.

---

Q: What equation relates the threshold potential difference $V$ of an LED to the wavelength $\lambda$ of the emitted light?
A: $eV = \frac{hc}{\lambda}$

---

Q: In the LED experiment to estimate the Planck constant, what graph should be plotted to find $h$?
A: Threshold potential difference $V$ against the inverse of wavelength, $1/\lambda$.

---

Q: What does the gradient of a graph of threshold potential difference $V$ against $1/\lambda$ represent in the LED experiment?
A: $\frac{hc}{e}$

---

Q: How can the accuracy of the Planck constant estimate using LEDs be improved?
A: By repeating the experiment with a variety of different coloured LEDs that emit different wavelengths of light.

---

Q: What is the photoelectric effect?
A: The release of electrons from the surface of a metal when electromagnetic radiation is shone on it.

---

Q: Describe the initial setup of a gold leaf electroscope used to demonstrate the photoelectric effect.
A: A zinc plate on top of a negatively charged stem, with a negatively charged piece of gold leaf attached to the stem, causing them to repel each other.

---

Q: What happens to a negatively charged gold leaf electroscope with a zinc plate when UV light is shone on the plate?
A: Free electrons are released from the surface, the negative charge is slowly lost, and the gold leaf gradually falls back to the stem.

---

Q: Why does visible light not cause the photoelectric effect in a gold leaf electroscope, regardless of intensity?
A: The frequency of visible light is below the threshold frequency, so individual photons do not have enough energy to overcome the work function of the metal.

---

Q: Why does low-intensity UV light instantaneously remove electrons from a zinc plate, whereas high-intensity visible light does not?
A: UV light has a higher frequency, meaning each photon has enough energy to overcome the work function, demonstrating the particulate nature of photons which the wave model cannot explain.

---

Q: What is the work function, $\phi$, of a metal?
A: The minimum energy required to free an electron from the surface of the metal.

---

Q: What term describes the minimum energy required to free an electron from the surface of a metal?
A: Work function.

---

Q: What is the threshold frequency in the context of the photoelectric effect?
A: The minimum frequency of electromagnetic radiation required to free electrons from the surface of a metal.

---

Q: What term describes the minimum frequency of electromagnetic radiation required to free electrons from a metal surface?
A: Threshold frequency.

---

Q: State Einstein’s photoelectric equation.
A: $hf = \phi + KE_{max}$

---

Q: In Einstein's photoelectric equation, what does $KE_{max}$ represent?
A: The maximum kinetic energy of the released electron.

---

Q: Why is the kinetic energy of a released electron a maximum value in Einstein's photoelectric equation?
A: Because some electrons may be closer to the nucleus, requiring more energy than the work function to be released, leaving less energy as kinetic energy.

---

Q: What happens to the rate of electron emission if the intensity of radiation is increased above the threshold frequency?
A: The rate of electron emission increases because there are more photons available to interact with the electrons.

---

Q: How can the kinetic energy of electrons released in the photoelectric effect be increased?
A: By increasing the frequency of the incident radiation further above the threshold frequency.

---

Q: What is wave-particle duality?
A: The concept that all matter and electromagnetic radiation can exhibit both wave and particle properties.

---

Q: State the de Broglie equation relating wavelength to momentum.
A: $\lambda = \frac{h}{p}$

---

Q: State the de Broglie equation relating wavelength to mass and velocity.
A: $\lambda = \frac{h}{mv}$

---

Q: Why is it harder to observe the wavelike properties of macroscopic objects?
A: As the mass of a particle increases, its de Broglie wavelength decreases, making wave properties harder to observe.

---

Q: What particle-like properties do electrons exhibit?
A: They have mass and charge, and can be accelerated and deflected by magnetic and electric fields.

---

Q: What wave-like property do electrons exhibit?
A: Diffraction.

---

Q: Describe the experiment that provides evidence for the wave nature of electrons.
A: A beam of electrons is fired at a thin piece of polycrystalline graphite, and the electrons are diffracted by the gaps between atoms, producing a diffraction pattern on a screen.

---

Q: Why is polycrystalline graphite used in the electron diffraction experiment?
A: It contains carbon atoms spread over many layers, providing gaps between atoms that act as diffraction slits for the electron beam.
