Q: State Newton's first law of motion.
A: An object will remain at rest or continue to travel with constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant force.

---

Q: State Newton's third law of motion.
A: When two objects interact, they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

---

Q: What are the four characteristics of the force pair described by Newton's third law?
A: They are of the same type, have the same magnitude, act on different objects, and act in opposite directions.

---

Q: Give an example of Newton's third law involving gravitational attraction.
A: The gravitational attraction of the Earth to a person is equal and opposite to the gravitational attraction of the person to the Earth.

---

Q: What are the four fundamental forces that can explain all forces in the universe?
A: Gravitational, electromagnetic, strong nuclear, and weak nuclear forces.

---

Q: State Newton's second law of motion in terms of momentum.
A: The net force acting on an object is directly proportional to the rate of change of momentum, and acts in the same direction.

---

Q: What is the mathematical expression for Newton's second law in terms of momentum?
A: $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$

---

Q: In the proportionality $F \propto \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}$, what is the value of the constant of proportionality in SI units?
A: 1

---

Q: Define linear momentum.
A: The product of an object's mass and its velocity.

---

Q: What is the formula for linear momentum?
A: $p = mv$

---

Q: What is the SI unit for linear momentum?
A: $kg \cdot m \cdot s^{-1}$

---

Q: Is linear momentum a scalar or a vector quantity?
A: It is a vector quantity, meaning both magnitude and direction must be considered.

---

Q: Why is $F = ma$ considered a special case of Newton's second law?
A: It is only true when the mass of the object remains constant during its motion.

---

Q: Show the derivation of $F = ma$ from the momentum form of Newton's second law.
A: $F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv - mu}{t} = m\left(\frac{v - u}{t}\right) = ma$

---

Q: Define the impulse of a force.
A: The product of the force and the time for which it acts, which is equal to the change in momentum.

---

Q: What is the formula for impulse?
A: $Impulse = F\Delta t = \Delta p = m(v - u)$

---

Q: What does the area under a force-time graph represent?
A: The impulse, which is equal to the change in momentum over that time duration.

---

Q: State the principle of conservation of momentum.
A: For a system of interacting objects, the total momentum in a specified direction remains constant, provided no external forces act on the system.

---

Q: What condition must be met for the total momentum of a system to be conserved?
A: There must be no external forces acting on the system.

---

Q: What defines a perfectly elastic collision?
A: A collision where the total kinetic energy of the system remains constant.

---

Q: What defines an inelastic collision?
A: A collision where some of the kinetic energy is lost to other forms, such as heat and sound energy.

---

Q: Which two quantities are conserved in both elastic and inelastic collisions?
A: Total momentum and total energy.

---

Q: What is the formula for the conservation of momentum in a one-dimensional collision between two objects?
A: $m_1u_1 + m_2u_2 = m_1v_1 + m_2v_2$

---

Q: How must direction be handled when calculating one-dimensional collisions using the conservation of momentum?
A: One direction of movement must be defined as negative, and negative signs must be used for objects traveling in that direction.

---

Q: How is the conservation of momentum applied to two-dimensional collisions?
A: The conservation of momentum must be considered for the x and y directions separately.
