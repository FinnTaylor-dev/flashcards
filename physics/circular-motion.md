name = "Circular Motion"
---

Q: What is the period ($T$) in circular motion?
A: The time, in seconds, taken for an object to travel a full circle.

---

Q: What is the frequency ($f$) in circular motion?
A: The number of full circles completed in one second, measured in Hertz (Hz).

---

Q: What is the relationship between period ($T$) and frequency ($f$)?
A: $f = \frac{1}{T}$

---

Q: What is angular velocity ($\omega$)?
A: The rate of change of angle of an object moving in a circle.

---

Q: What is the formula for angular velocity ($\omega$) in terms of angle ($\theta$) and time ($t$)?
A: $\omega = \frac{\theta}{t}$

---

Q: What are the units of angular velocity?
A: rad s$^{-1}$ (radians per second).

---

Q: What is the formula for angular velocity ($\omega$) in terms of period ($T$)?
A: $\omega = \frac{2\pi}{T}$

---

Q: What is the formula for angular velocity ($\omega$) in terms of frequency ($f$)?
A: $\omega = 2\pi f$

---

Q: Why is an object moving in a circle at a constant speed still accelerating?
A: Its direction is constantly changing, meaning its velocity (a vector quantity) is constantly changing.

---

Q: What is centripetal force?
A: The net force which acts perpendicular to the direction of the velocity, towards the centre of the circle.

---

Q: In what direction does the centripetal force act relative to the velocity?
A: Perpendicular to the direction of the velocity, towards the centre of the circle.

---

Q: Is centripetal force a new, separate type of force?
A: No, it is the resultant (net) force acting towards the centre of the circle, which can be provided by forces such as gravity, friction, or tension.

---

Q: What is the formula for the speed ($v$) of an object moving in a circle of radius $r$ and period $T$?
A: $v = \frac{2\pi r}{T}$

---

Q: What is the formula for the speed ($v$) of an object in circular motion in terms of angular velocity ($\omega$) and radius ($r$)?
A: $v = \omega r$

---

Q: What is the formula for centripetal acceleration ($a$) in terms of speed ($v$) and radius ($r$)?
A: $a = \frac{v^2}{r}$

---

Q: What is the formula for centripetal acceleration ($a$) in terms of angular velocity ($\omega$) and radius ($r$)?
A: $a = \omega^2 r$

---

C: The centripetal acceleration of an object moving in a circle is given by the formula [$a = \frac{v^2}{r}$] or [$a = \omega^2 r$].

---

Q: What is the formula for centripetal force ($F$) in terms of mass ($m$), speed ($v$), and radius ($r$)?
A: $F = \frac{m v^2}{r}$

---

Q: What is the formula for centripetal force ($F$) in terms of mass ($m$), angular velocity ($\omega$), and radius ($r$)?
A: $F = m \omega^2 r$

---

Q: Give three examples of forces that can provide centripetal force.
A: Gravitational fields, friction, and tension in a rope.

---

Q: In the bung and string circular motion experiment, what provides the centripetal force?
A: The tension in the string, which is equal to the weight of the suspended mass ($Mg$).

---

Q: In the circular motion experiment with a bung of mass $m$ and a suspended weight of mass $M$, what is the equation equating centripetal force and the weight?
A: $Mg = \frac{m v^2}{r}$

---

Q: In the circular motion experiment, what graph is plotted to verify the relationship between velocity and mass, and what is its expected shape?
A: $v^2$ plotted against $M$, which should produce a straight line graph passing through the origin.

---
