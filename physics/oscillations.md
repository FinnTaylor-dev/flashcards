Q: What is displacement ($x$) in the context of oscillations?
A: The distance from the equilibrium position.

---

Q: What term describes the distance from the equilibrium position in an oscillation?
A: Displacement ($x$).

---

Q: What is the amplitude ($A$) of an oscillation?
A: The maximum displacement from the equilibrium position.

---

Q: What is the period ($T$) of an oscillation?
A: The time taken to complete one full oscillation.

---

Q: What is the frequency ($f$) of an oscillation?
A: The number of oscillations per unit time.

---

Q: What is the mathematical relationship between frequency ($f$) and period ($T$)?
A: They are reciprocals of each other, expressed as $f = \frac{1}{T}$.

---

Q: What is the formula for the period ($T$) in terms of frequency ($f$)?
A: $T = \frac{1}{f}$.

---

Q: What is phase difference ($\phi$) between two oscillating objects?
A: The fraction of an oscillation between their positions, given by the formula $\phi = \frac{\Delta t}{T} \times 2\pi$.

---

Q: What is angular frequency ($\omega$)?
A: The rate of change of angular position, given by the formula $\omega = 2\pi f$.

---

Q: What is the formula for angular frequency ($\omega$) in terms of frequency ($f$)?
A: $\omega = 2\pi f$.

Q: What is the defining condition for simple harmonic motion?
A: The acceleration of the oscillator is directly proportional to the displacement from the equilibrium position and acts towards the equilibrium position.

Q: What is the key equation for simple harmonic motion?
A: $a = -\omega^2 x$.

---

Q: In the simple harmonic motion equation $a = -\omega^2 x$, what does the negative sign indicate?
A: It indicates that the direction of acceleration is always towards the equilibrium position, which is opposite to the direction of displacement.

---

Q: What does it mean for an oscillator in simple harmonic motion to be isochronous?
A: The period of the oscillation is independent of the amplitude.

---

Q: How can the accuracy of measuring the period of an oscillator be increased when using a stopwatch?
A: By measuring the time taken for 10 oscillations and dividing this total time by 10.

---

Q: Where should a fiducial marker be placed when timing an oscillator, and what is its purpose?
A: It is normally placed at the equilibrium position to serve as a clear reference point to start and stop timings.

Q: What is the equation for the velocity ($v$) of an oscillator at a given displacement ($x$) in simple harmonic motion?
A: $v = \pm \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$.

---

Q: What is the formula for the maximum velocity ($v_{max}$) of an oscillator in simple harmonic motion?
A: $v_{max} = \omega A$.

---

Q: How is the maximum velocity formula $v_{max} = \omega A$ derived from the general velocity equation?
A: Maximum velocity occurs at the equilibrium position where displacement $x = 0$. Substituting $x = 0$ into $v = \pm \omega \sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$ yields $v = \pm \omega \sqrt{A^2} = \omega A$.

C: [Damping] is the process by which the amplitude of oscillations decreases over time due to energy loss to resistive forces such as drag or friction.

---

Q: What characterizes light damping in an oscillating system?
A: The amplitude decreases exponentially over time, such as a pendulum oscillating in air.

---

Q: What characterizes heavy damping in an oscillating system?
A: The amplitude decreases dramatically, such as a pendulum oscillating in water.

---

Q: What characterizes critical damping in an oscillating system?
A: The object stops before one full oscillation is completed, such as a pendulum oscillating in treacle.

---

Q: What is free oscillation?
A: When an object oscillates without any external forces being applied, oscillating at its natural frequency.

---

Q: What is forced oscillation?
A: When a periodic driving force is applied to an object, causing it to oscillate at the driving frequency.

---

C: [Resonance] occurs when the [driving frequency] of an external force applied to an object is the same as the [natural frequency] of the object.

---

Q: What happens to the amplitude of an undamped system when it is at resonance?
A: The amplitude will rapidly increase and continue to increase until the system fails.

---

Q: How does increased damping affect the resonance of a system?
A: It decreases the amplitude at all frequencies, and the maximum amplitude occurs at a lower frequency.

---

Q: Describe a basic experimental setup to investigate resonance.
A: A mass is suspended between two springs attached to an oscillation generator, with a millimetre ruler placed parallel to the spring-mass system to record the amplitude.

---

Q: What is the procedure for investigating resonance using a mass-spring system and an oscillation generator?
A: The driver frequency of the generator is slowly increased from zero. The mass will oscillate with increasing amplitude, reaching a maximum when the driver frequency matches the natural frequency of the system, and then the amplitude will decrease as the frequency is increased further.

---

Q: How can the accuracy of amplitude measurement be improved when investigating resonance experimentally?
A: By filming the system and recording the amplitude value from video stills, as it can be difficult to determine the amplitude whilst the mass is actively oscillating.
